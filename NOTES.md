Big canvases are fast now. A board with hundreds of notes and images and dozens of live web frames draws only what is on screen, plus half a screen around it. Live frames load only when they are on screen and big enough to read, a few at a time; a frame that is let go shows a snapshot of its site under its title bar, so the board still reads as the real sites. Images drawn small use a small copy, and notes and text far zoomed out draw as simple blocks. Agents read a big canvas a page at a time: `canvas_read` takes `overview`, `frame`, `region`, `ids`, `types`, `query`, `limit` and `cursor`, and canvas tools no longer fail on a canvas tab that is asleep, just restored or slow to load. Ctrl+T lists Open Tabs, then History, then Search, and Tab and Shift+Tab jump between the sections. Closing a Saved tab keeps it in Saved, as in Arc; only the row's close button removes it. You can also drag a tab into Saved.

**Big canvases.** Only the shapes near the view are drawn, and panning and zooming no longer re-render every shape. Live web frames have a budget: one loads after the view has been still for a moment, only when it is on screen and wide enough to read, nearest the middle first. A frame you are using is never let go. A frame that is let go keeps a snapshot of its site, which Copper takes from the page while the frame is live. Below a legible zoom, notes, text, checklists and timelines draw as a colour block with a heading line, and frame titles stay readable. Images are decoded lazily and drawn from a small copy when shown much smaller than their real size. At normal zoom the board looks as it did.

**Agents and canvases.** `canvas_read` on a big canvas answers in pages: at most `limit` shapes (default 200) and about 48 KB each, frames first, then by position. `next` continues, and edits made between pages never repeat or skip a shape. `overview: true` gives counts by type, the bounds, the frames with their ids and titles, and comment counts, without shapes. `frame`, `region`, `ids`, `types` and `query` narrow a read. A canvas tab restored asleep used to rebuild its page over and over under an agent and fail after about 20 seconds. Canvas tools now wake it once and keep it awake while they work. They wait for the page in stages for up to a minute, and if one ever stalls, the error names it. `canvas_screenshot` waits up to 2.5 seconds for the images and web frames on screen to load, so a board that was asleep no longer comes back with blank pictures, and it says what was still loading if they took longer.

**Tabs.** In the Ctrl+T field, results come in sections in a fixed order: Open Tabs, History, then Search. The first row is highlighted, so Return opens the best open tab or page you have visited. A typed address still opens on Return. Tab and Shift+Tab move to the next or previous section, the arrow keys move row by row, and → or End takes the offered completion. Ctrl+W, Close Tab and an agent's close all put a Saved tab to sleep: it stays in Saved, in its place, with its title and icon. Only the × on the row (or Unsave in its menu) takes it out of Saved, and Ctrl+Shift+T puts it back where it was. Drag any tab up into Saved to keep it, and a line shows where it will land. When Saved is empty, a drop place still appears.

**Addons.** Ctrl+E opens the Agent sidebar on the right of the window, full height; drag its edge to make it wider. What it shows comes from your Copper Cloud: a small web app with its own storage and settings, running in a view of its own with a strict content policy. A new addon or version is checked and staged first; Settings › Updates and a toast then say "Update available", and Install loads it at once, without a relaunch. Settings › Addons lists them, turns one off on this computer or removes it, and checks for updates. Without an addon, the sidebar says so and points to Settings. Developers can run a folder directly with `COPPER_ADDONS_DEV_DIR` in a test world (docs/addons.md).
